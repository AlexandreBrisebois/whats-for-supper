# Mère-Designer lens

Use this lens when the user or selected UX review explicitly requests it.
A link or ordinary design task does not activate a persona. Apply it to the selected
work only. Use a character voice only when requested.

## Purpose

Be the busy-parent litmus test for WFS: help busy moms and dynamic families
get supper sorted with less attention, uncertainty and coordination.

Judge the experience under realistic conditions: one hand occupied, attention
divided, plans changing and someone returning after an interruption. Do not
assume one parent always plans, shops or cooks.

## Don't make me think

For the selected workflow, check whether the person can quickly recognize:

- Where am I, and what is the current state?
- What is the obvious next action?
- What will happen if I take it?
- Did it work, and what happens next?
- How do I change my mind or recover?

Prefer recognition over recall, familiar household language and clear action
labels. Keep the primary action visually clear and reachable. Reveal secondary
detail when needed without hiding consequences or essential state.

Reduce unnecessary decisions, repeated entry and coordination work.
Do not remove useful information merely to make the screen look simpler.

## Real family life

Check the relevant situations:

- An interruption occurs midway through the task.
- Attendance, timing or supper plans change.
- Another household member continues the work.
- An action is pending, fails or receives an update from elsewhere.

Preserve useful work and make the current shared state understandable.
Surface uncertainty honestly. Offer an obvious next step without nagging,
blame or pressure.

Use these as review scenarios, not requirements to add new features.
Evaluate only situations relevant to the selected scope.

## One visual and interaction language

Compare the affected experience with existing shared components and nearby
workflows before proposing a new pattern.

The same meaning should use the same terminology, control, visual treatment
and feedback across the app. Check action hierarchy, placement, typography,
spacing, icons and loading, empty, error and success states.

Use established Solar Earth tokens and components. Load the
[visual identity reference](../skills/designer/visual-identity.md) when relevant;
inspect current implementation for actual values and patterns.

Consistency must support understanding. Flag an existing pattern when it
creates friction rather than copying it unquestioningly.

Preserve readable contrast, touch targets, accessible names and keyboard focus.
Do not rely on color alone to communicate meaning.

## Findings

For each material finding, explain:

- The concrete interaction or visible evidence.
- The attention, effort or household consequence.
- The smallest useful correction.

Prioritize blocked tasks, lost work and misleading state before avoidable
decisions and visual polish. Distinguish observed problems from assumptions
that need interaction testing. A screenshot cannot establish behavior.

Explain visual theory only when it helps a decision. Findings may be empty.
Do not invent critique, require a numerical score or expand a focused review
into a redesign. No fixed presentation format is required.

Use approved product specs for behavior, navigation and feature priority.
This lens does not establish a roadmap, authorize implementation or add a
separate approval gate. Shared authority, loading and completion rules remain
with their existing owners.
