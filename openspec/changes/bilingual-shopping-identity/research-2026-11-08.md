# Ingredient vocabulary research — 2026-11-08

Research evidence and nonnormative recommendations only. This note does not adopt
a dataset, approve vocabulary, change product requirements or establish new design
commitments. Existing specifications and their approval gates remain authoritative.

## Start with existing ingredient knowledge

There is already a consolidation plan: existing category records and recipe-derived
terms can feed reviewed associations with app-owned food identities. External
references and model-assisted drafts help qualify gaps; they are not a replacement
ingredient database or a separately adopted vocabulary.

The existing [bilingual design](design.md) proposes saved, versioned mappings,
reviewed exact aliases and EN/FR labels, conservative fallback for unknown terms,
and protection of human choices. The shared [proposed food/category contracts](../canonical-ingredient-correction/contracts.md#food-identity-and-category-correction)
describe preserving existing category choices during a future identity cutover,
including unresolved conflicts rather than a last-write-wins merge. These plans
must not be mistaken for completed implementation.

## Local evidence, not live database counts

Read-only inspection of the local `data/ingredient-categories.csv` found **711
records**, with source labels **697 `llm` and 14 `manual`**. These are stored
provenance labels only: this inspection did not establish who created the manual
records, when they were imported or whether they reflect this user's corrections.
Examples include separate olive oil/huile d'olive records and distinct cream fat
percentages. They are useful inputs for review, not automatic equivalence facts.

The local `data/recipes/*/recipe.json` snapshot contained **198 files, 2,430
ingredient-line occurrences and 1,463 distinct verbatim lines**, with no JSON parse
errors. All files were included regardless of readiness or deletion status. The
rolling disk read was not a transactionally consistent service snapshot. Neither
these numbers nor the category CSV counts establish current live database contents
or production coverage.

Common lines included `Huile`, `Sel et poivre (S-P)`, `Salt`, `Pepper`,
`Huile d'olive` and `Olive Oil`; a mixed line such as `3 Tablespoon Beurre` shows why
whole-line language inference is insufficient. Alternatives such as
`Échalote française (ou oignon)` must retain choice semantics. Frequency prioritizes
review; it does not validate a translation. Recipe content and the per-file hash
manifest remain in local research scratch artifacts rather than a tracked dataset.

The legacy 2015 guide cannot substitute for selected 2026 release terms. |
| [culinary-dictionary.com](https://culinary-dictionary.com/) | Menu-oriented collection mixing food, dishes, methods and equipment, with some regional markers and no structured Canadian coverage observed. | OQLF Grand dictionnaire terminologique | Useful Canadian French sense and regional evidence. The [reference-service description](https://vitrinelinguistique.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/a-propos-de-la-vitrine-linguistique/offre-de-services-linguistiques) describes generally bilingual terminology records. The [copyright notice](https://www.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/coordonnees.html) requires authorization for reproduction. No bulk permission was obtained. Consult/cite rather than importing the collection. |
| [TERMIUM Plus](https://www.btb.termiumplus.gc.ca/tpv2alpha/alpha-fra.html?lang=eng) | Canadian multilingual terminology bank with subject/context distinctions. Bulk export/reuse rights were not established here; government ownership alone does not establish open licensing for every record. |

A concrete quality concern: culinary-dictionary.com pairs “pieuvre” with
“cuttlefish.” [TERMIUM's octopus entry](https://www.btb.termiumplus.gc.ca/tpv2alpha/alpha-eng.html?fchrcrdnm=1&i=&index=ent&lang=eng&sg_kp_wet=1243258&srchtxt=OCTOPUS)
associates octopus with pieuvre/poulpe, while [OQLF's seiche entry](https://vitrinelinguistique.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/fiche-gdt/fiche/1198916/seiche)
identifies cuttlefish. This is a demonstrated counterexample to an exact crosswalk,
not a measured website error rate.

[OQLF's oignon vert entry](https://vitrinelinguistique.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/fiche-gdt/fiche/2083674/oignon-vert)
also documents Quebec's overlapping use of échalote and oignon vert. Bare échalote
cannot safely become a global alias for either shallot or green onion. Locale is
useful evidence, but does not prove the intended sense of an individual recipe.

## Nonnormative qualification suggestions

Model knowledge can draft common pairs quickly, but this research did not compare
model accuracy against a gold standard or establish superiority over references.
Original drafts such as olive oil/huile d'olive and ground beef/bœuf haché remain
**provisional, unreviewed and unadopted**. A generated term has no verified source
provenance merely because it sounds plausible. Do not invent source citations.

A small reviewed set built from existing category keys and recipe terms would be
easier to inspect and reverse than a broad imported crosswalk. Its trade-off is
incomplete coverage and ongoing review. Corpus-driven generation improves relevance
but can amplify extraction mistakes; large external datasets add qualifier parsing,
rights and refresh costs. External references can resolve gaps without becoming
application dependencies.

For a future qualification exercise, consider recording original term, locale,
proposed identity, exact-versus-related relation, purchase qualifiers, evidence,
source/version/rights status and reviewer decision. Keep candidates and rejected
relations outside any runtime exact-alias set. This is a suggested review ledger,
not a new API/schema requirement or a parallel owner of the shared contracts.

Review exact translations separately from broader categories, substitutions,
preparation and preservation differences. Difficult cases include:

- Fresh/canned tomato and tomato paste; salted/unsalted butter; wheat/gluten-free
  flour: preserve product requirements rather than merging related foods.
- Pepper/poivre/poivron/piment; coriander leaf/seed; unspecified cream: retain
  ambiguity or qualifiers rather than choosing a likely sense.
- Diced/sliced onion: preparation can share a purchase concept only with evidence
  that it is kitchen preparation, rather than a purchased pre-cut product.
- Garlic clove/minced garlic/powder, broth/concentrate and package sizes: translation
  alone does not prove equivalent products, dilution or quantity conversions.
- Combined seasoning and “or” alternatives: neither is an alias for one constituent.

A bounded follow-up could review up to 50 concepts drawn from existing records,
then evaluate held-out recipe occurrences and adversarial cases. Report correct
accepted mappings, false merges, abstentions and conflicts separately, with explicit
denominators and language/form breakdowns. Zero observed false merges in a pilot
would be a useful review gate, not a population guarantee or an adopted acceptance
threshold. Preserve manual category choices and expose conflicts during any future
mapping review; do not infer identity from a shared category.

If a dataset subset is proposed for adoption, its exact package/version/hash,
licence and attribution evidence still need qualification. The existing plan's
runtime goals—saved mappings, deterministic groceries, protected corrections and
no model calls on read/recompute—remain future implementation work requiring their
own approval and tests. No runtime tests or production flows were executed for this
research, and no application behavior or data was changed.
