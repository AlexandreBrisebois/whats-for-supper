# What’s for Supper North Star

What’s for Supper helps a household turn the recurring question of what to eat into something they choose together and look forward to. It brings their own recipes, tastes and traditions into everyday life. It carries the coordination work without taking away their choices, makes room for plans to change, and helps them return to life with fewer loose ends.

“That’s one less thing weighing on me. We chose this together, it feels like us, and I’m looking forward to supper.”

Success is not more time spent in the app. It is a household that feels supported, knows what happens next, and can put the app down with confidence.

The emotional anchors are relief, togetherness, belonging and anticipation.
The principles and examples below express intended experience, not a certification
of current behavior or permission to add features.

## Five principles

### Carry the work, preserve choice

Take on repetitive coordination while keeping people in control of meaningful decisions. Make it clear what is saved, pending, shared or ready. Let people leave a task with confidence rather than supervise every step.

**Capture example:** Photograph a recipe, optionally choose a cooked-meal photo or add a note or the family’s appreciation, then save. The intended feeling is “save and forget”: return to life while the app handles the background work. Keep the original available, make failures actionable, and support checking extracted ingredients and instructions before cooking without making that check a prerequisite to saving. Do not promise instant or infallible extraction.

### Make participation worthwhile, not endless

Help household members contribute enough to make a useful choice together. Participation should reduce the burden on everyone, not turn one person into the permanent coordinator or require everybody to work through the whole collection.

**Voting example:** When there are enough useful matches, make the outcome and next step understandable and allow a quick exit. Distinguish matched suggestions from meals actually assigned to the plan. Do not imply that a celebration has locked the week or completed work that remains undone.

### Make room for real life

Plans change. People arrive late, ingredients are missing, energy runs low, and sometimes supper comes from somewhere else. Adapting should feel ordinary, without blame or a sense of failure.

**Pivot example:** Choosing another recipe, takeout or eating out should make clear what happens to the displaced meal and its grocery ingredients. Preserve useful work and explain the consequences of a change. Do not imply that the app knows what is in the cupboard or can guarantee that nothing will be wasted.

### Make it feel like their home

Build around the household’s own recipes, tastes, food needs, budget and traditions. Leave room for inherited favourites and new discoveries. Togetherness means meaningful participation; belonging means recognising something of ourselves in what we choose. Neither requires an ideal household or assumes who plans, shops or cooks.

### There are no dead ends in the app

At an empty state, interruption, failure or completed task, help people understand where they are and what they can do next. That next step may be retrying, changing course, returning later or simply finishing. Do not invent another task just to keep someone engaged.

## Trust and accessibility throughout

Relief depends on honest expectations. Distinguish original recipe evidence, real meal photos and generated illustrations. Explain external AI processing and material limitations. Marketing should promise only what the experience and supporting evidence can justify.

Make participation understandable and accessible across the journey. Use Canadian accessibility guidance as the primary practical reference and WCAG 2.2 AA as the chosen web target, without implying certification. Clear language, visible state, keyboard access and recoverable errors support the same confidence as warm visual design.

## Character to preserve

Keep Solar Earth warm, distinctive and beautiful. Let typography, colour, composition and earned moments of delight support clarity and anticipation. Reuse established components and tokens; do not turn particular colours, blur values or animation into universal requirements.

Preserve progress that reduces household work: grocery extras, quick voting convergence, recipe rotation through dreaming and PDF import. Alex identified these as meaningful gains in October 2026; that reported experience guides preservation, not claims of independently verified behavior.
